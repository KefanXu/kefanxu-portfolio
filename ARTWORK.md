## Hero: care-ecology

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: original hero artwork for a PhD researcher's academic website about human-centered computing, health informatics, care and life transitions. Create a refined sculptural still life on a seamless PURE WHITE #ffffff background. Three smooth organic river-stone forms in a carefully balanced interconnected arrangement: one large irregular ivory plaster loop embracing open space, one smaller deep muted teal #1F6F66 rounded arch leaning gently beside it, one small pale sage pebble. A fine dark teal thread curves naturally through the openings, visually connecting the forms and loosely tracing an orbit on the white ground. Material is tactile matte ceramic and honed stone with delicate grain; very soft contact shadows. Sophisticated museum-quality contemporary sculpture, serene and human, not corporate stock graphics, not sci-fi. Isometric-ish front three-quarter view with a natural 70mm lens; entire composition centered in square image, objects occupy about 70% of canvas, generous white margins, soft daylight from upper left. Sculptural depth, balanced asymmetry, white background all the way to the outer edges. No text, letters, logos, watermarks, humans, faces, extra props, gradients in background or frame.

## Current hero presentation

The main hero now displays `public/images/care-ecology.webp` directly and persistently. It is the original generated still life documented above, with one large ivory loop, a smaller teal loop, a sage pebble, and a fine connecting thread. The September 15 revision removes the transition to WebGL so the preferred composition is visible from the first paint onward.

The earlier procedural Three.js experiment remains in `heroSculptureScene.ts`, `ceramicMaterials.ts`, and `sculptureGeometry.ts` for reference, but the homepage no longer imports or loads it.

The research-perspective SVG uses abstract ivory and sage forms, teal orbits, and pebble satellites. The practice illustrations use curved sheets, interlocking loops, and an orbital sculpture. These original code illustrations move with scroll position and do not depict people, interfaces, or measured study data.

The nine research covers use original generated ceramic still lifes, with live HTML titles and metadata in `ResearchBook.tsx`. The book-opening endpaper is an original abstract orbital composition. No artwork was copied from the reference websites.

Motion direction was informed by reviewing [Stripe Press](https://press.stripe.com/), [Max Milkin](https://maxmilkin.com/), and [Olha Lazarieva](https://olhalazarieva.com/): physical book transitions, sculptural movement, and editorial text reveals, adapted to the portfolio's white background and academic content.

Published interface figures in the project reader are documented separately in `ASSETS.md`; missing original project images have explicit placeholders.
